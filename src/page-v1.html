NPCI product statistics · a dataviz exercise

UPI, in six charts

Same public tables, six decisions. Each chart ships the right form, and a toggle that shows the common mistake so you can feel the difference instead of reading about it.

0

Before any chart: is it even a chart?

October 2026, the latest month NPCI has published. Four numbers, each against the same month a year ago.

Lesson

A single current value is a stat tile, not a one-bar bar chart. The number is the chart. The sparkline is context, not a second chart: twelve points in the de-emphasis gray, the current period in the accent.

Note the one number nobody publishes directly: average ticket size. It is value divided by volume. Chapter 2 is about that number.

1

Ten years of volume: linear hides the first four

Monthly UPI transactions, April 2016 to October 2026. One series, one hue, no legend.

Monthly transactions
millions per month

Lesson

Linear answers "how big is it now". Log answers "how fast was it growing". On linear the first four years are a flat line glued to the baseline, and demonetisation in November 2016 is invisible. Switch to log and the same data shows the three phases: the 2017 launch spike, a steady doubling-ish every year, then the slope flattening from 2023 as the base gets large.

Rule of thumb: if the max is more than about 100× the min and the question is about rate, use log. Label the ticks in plain units so nobody has to read exponents.

2

The number that is not in the table

Average transaction size, in rupees. Value divided by volume, every month since April 2017.

Average ticket size
₹ per transaction

Lesson

Derive before you draw. NPCI publishes volume and value. Neither is the story. Their ratio is: the average UPI payment has shrunk by more than half as the network moved from occasional bank-transfer-sized payments to chai-sized ones. Chapter 3 shows why.

Also notice the y-axis starts at zero. A ratio line that starts at zero is honest about how much fell; a truncated axis would make a 60% decline look like a cliff to nothing.

3

Person-to-person vs person-to-merchant: count or share?

The same split, four ways. NPCI breaks out P2P (sending money to a person) and P2M (paying a merchant) from January 2022.

Transactions by type
millions per month

Lesson

Stacked count shows growth. Stacked share shows the mix. You usually need both, in that order. On count, both segments grow and the eye reads "everything is up". On share, the orange band crosses the midline: merchant payments went from under half of transactions to nearly two-thirds. Flip to Value and the picture inverts, because a merchant payment is a fraction of the size of a transfer to a friend.

Two series, so colour alone is fine, but the legend is still there. A 2px gap in the surface colour separates the segments; no outlines.

4

App share: emphasis beats a rainbow

Share of monthly UPI transactions by app, January 2022 to September 2026. Eight apps, log scale, so a 1.7% line can share a plot with a 46% line.

Share of transactions by app
% of monthly volume, log scale

Apps shown: the eight that appear most in NPCI's monthly top-8. A gap means the app fell outside the top 8 that month. Apps with no published share before 2022 are not shown.

Lesson

Decide what the story is, then colour only that. The duopoly is not news: PhonePe and Google Pay have held about 80% the whole time. The story is the challengers: Paytm losing a third of its share after RBI's curbs on Paytm Payments Bank in early 2024, and Navi and super.money appearing from nothing in mid-2024 and climbing. Three accent hues on those, the rest in gray with a direct label. Click "Show the mistake" and watch eight equal hues turn the same chart into a legend-matching puzzle.

Colour follows the entity. Toggling never repaints a survivor: PhonePe is the same gray in both modes, Navi is the same blue.

5

Where the transactions are: ranked bars, not a map

September 2026 volume by state. One hue, largest first, and the awkward part of the data left visible.

Transactions by state, Sep 2026
millions, top 15 of 37 states and union territories

Gray rows are not states: the unclassified remainder, one district placed in the ranking for scale, and the folded tail.

Lesson

A choropleth would colour Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh by area, not by payments. Ranked bars compare the values exactly, on one baseline, and let you read that one city district (Bengaluru Urban) alone out-transacts every state except the top five.

Do not hide the hole in the data. 44% of September's transactions are "Unclassified" in NPCI's state table. Dropping that row would make the state shares look twice as confident as they are. It stays at the top, in gray, with its number.

6

What people buy: two measures, one axis each

Merchant categories in September 2026. How many payments versus how large each payment is.

Merchant categories: count vs ticket size
29 categories NPCI publishes · both axes log

Lesson

Two measures of different scale is the moment people reach for a dual-axis chart. Do not. Two y-scales on one plot invent a correlation by how they happen to line up. Give each measure its own axis in a scatter and the shape is the data: groceries are the most frequent purchase and among the smallest; securities brokers and debt collectors are rare and huge; cigarette shops are the cheapest thing on the network.

Label a handful of points, not all 29. Each dot has a 24px hit area so hover works without aiming, and the table carries every value.

Source: NPCI UPI product statistics and ecosystem statistics, fetched 3 November 2026. Product statistics run to October 2026; ecosystem tables (apps, states, merchant categories) lag by a month and run to September 2026. Value is in crore rupees as published; ₹1 lakh crore = ₹1 trillion.

Method: the dataviz skill's procedure. Form first, colour by job, palette validated for colour-vision deficiency in light and dark, thin marks, legend for two or more series, hover layer, table view for every chart.
